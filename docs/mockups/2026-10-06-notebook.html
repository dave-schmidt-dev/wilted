Notebook: notes, clips and ideas from what you listen to and read

David's idea, 2026-10-06: a place to log notes, clips and ideas from podcasts, books and articles, to be referenced later by a person or an AI, and usable to train discovery. Static mockup, synthetic data. Nothing here is built; David selects an option or defers the area.

Grounded in what exists: transcripts with follow-along, and per-episode Notes. A clip is a transcript time range, the listener's note, and its source (podcast, article or audiobook). Hands-free capture covers listening in the car: a Siri or Watch "save that" grabs the last ~30 seconds.

Option A: Notebook as its own destination

A fourth place in the sidebar (Mac) and a fourth destination on iPhone. Capture lives on the player; browsing, search and export live in the Notebook.

Hands-free (car, walking): "Hey Siri, save that" or a Watch tap saves the last ~30 s of the playing item with no note, and the phone says "Saved to Notebook" aloud. The clip is added to the Notebook marked "Unreviewed" so a note can be added later.
Data contract
  • NotebookEntry: id, createdAt, kind clip|note|idea, sourceKind podcast|article|audiobook, sourceItemID, sourceRevision, startSeconds, endSeconds (nil for note/idea), textExcerpt, note, tags [String], capturedOn mac|iphone|watch|siri, reviewed Bool.
  • Capture window: the last 30 s ending at the current position, snapped to transcript segment boundaries. With no transcript yet, store the time range only; textExcerpt fills in when the transcript arrives.
  • Articles use a paragraph range instead of seconds. Entries keep the source title and URL so they survive the item being removed from the Larder.
  • Synced through the existing private CloudKit path as a new record type; deleting an entry writes a tombstone. Offline capture queues and syncs later.
  • Export: Markdown (one file, grouped by day, each entry with source, time range, excerpt and note) and JSON (schemaVersion, entries[] as above). Export is a user action; nothing leaves the device or private iCloud without it.
  • Discovery: the JSON is the training input. Any ranking that learns from it runs locally; this mockup proposes no server upload.

Option B: Notebook as a view over per-episode Notes

No new destination. Notes stay on the episode; clips become timestamped notes. "All notes" is a Larder filter and a Search scope, and export hangs off it.

Hands-free: "Hey Siri, save that" or a Watch tap adds a ~30 s clip to the playing episode's Notes. Articles and books clip to their own Notes the same way.
Data contract
  • Same NotebookEntry fields as A, stored as children of the episode's existing Notes record instead of a separate record type.
  • Cross-source search and export read the Notes of every item; an entry is lost with its item unless the item is kept (trade-off against A).
  • Export: the same Markdown and JSON, built on demand from every item that has Notes.
  • Needs a "Has notes" Larder filter and a Search scope; no new navigation destination.

Recommendation: A. The idea is a place that outlives any one item and feeds discovery, which a view over per-episode Notes cannot do once an item is removed. B is smaller if David wants the Notes he already has to simply become searchable.

Open questions for David: (1) Should articles and books clip by paragraph, or only podcasts and audiobooks by time? (2) Is local-only discovery training acceptable, or should the JSON also be exportable to an AI service on request? (3) Does a clip keep a short audio snippet, or only transcript text and the time range?
