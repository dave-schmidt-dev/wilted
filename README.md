# wilted

Local-first native Mac/iOS personal audio system: the Mac is the daily-use producer and player, while the deferred iOS listener receives and plays qualified media.

Mac **Ad here** remains available for a loaded podcast even when its cut timeline is missing. Cut-specific verdicts still require a known cut. A report without a timeline keeps the heard revision and played position without guessing an original timestamp.

Background sync uses metadata-only library projections and exact transcript reads. Unchanged publication and media-offer projections reuse their prior values, while account changes, changed eligibility and failed sends remain retryable. Timestamp parsing and formatting share locked formatters. Player surfaces share one readout clock that stops while paused; transport events still refresh immediately. Log archiving queries only the recent overlap after its first written entry, and unchanged media accounting skips replacement only after confirmed durable success. These are source behaviors. Installed CPU and memory improvements require a fresh measurement of the installed build.

Mac transcript, preparation cuts and post-start queue presentation run outside the serialized audio-command tail. Presentation uses the exact loaded item/revision and selection generation. Pause retains its request, superseded results cannot publish, and close cancels and joins the model ledger. Synthetic traces separate backend-call timing from actor admission, fetch and decode. The qualified 50ms pre-audio target does not measure rendered or audible latency (timings only measure dispatch-to-audio-call, never audible proof).

Podcast Remove and Mark completed now use one retained-media manual finish. Both retire the episode from Larder and active Feeds without moving its saved playhead or increasing listening totals. Mac Undo restores receipt-owned prior completion, decision provenance and queue placement within 10 seconds. Freed automatic capacity stays reserved until the receipt is durably sealed, and restart rebuilds expiry and retries the oldest waiting admission. A later explicit Restore may exceed the kept limit without evicting another episode; rule and policy Keep then wait for capacity. Started transfers and newer requests keep running; explicit Restore preserves prior listening while recording a fresh owner Keep choice; later edits remain authoritative and Undo never starts audio. V19 adds durable receipts while preserving V18 models. Phone queue withdrawal stops playback but retains exact preparation proof for offline Restore; dismissed or changed preparation still revokes it. V20 reversible confirmed same-feed association keeps all raw identities, media, checkpoints, and queue slots, folds display, and expands logical actions and capacity; original phone timestamps and newest choice guards are preserved, preferring exact-source handoff first and alternate only with matching prepared hash, bytes, media type, revisions, and duration within 0.05 seconds, without destructive merge or live association application. Ads are cut only on a validated reply from the online labeller (charter W-INV-013, W-INV-042). The worker transcribes the episode, the Mac requests labelling for a confirmed show, and the worker cuts the labelled breaks. A show that is not sent keeps its ads: the original audio is kept whole and the episode carries a durable warning `original audio includes ads: <reason sentence>`. The fixed reasons are online ad finding off, no provider key saved, and show not confirmed. A confirmed show at the daily request limit is not cut locally: its episode waits for the next local midnight when that falls inside the wait limit, and otherwise keeps the whole original audio with a warning that the daily limit was reached. An uncertain labelling outcome, a cue digest mismatch, or a cut over the share ceiling keeps the proven original audio playable and records a durable warning; genuine audio, render, or proof errors remain fatal.

When a feed lists an entry that looks like an episode already in the library (same feed, same title ignoring case and spacing but not punctuation, publication times within 60 seconds, durations within 5 seconds, or equal known byte counts when both durations are missing; a clearly different length is not flagged), refresh saves it as its own row with a **Possible duplicate of …** badge in Feeds and the Larder, shown even while it is active. One central check holds a flagged row out of every automatic download and preparation path (the download-everything and prepare-everything settings, per-feed switches, download after Keep, Keep rules and Apply Rules, recovery and pipeline invalidation, and download arrival), and blocks what it cannot clear if the flags cannot be read. Skip rules never retire a flagged row. Manual download and Prepare work as usual. **Same episode** skips or removes the newcomer; **Different** clears the flag and catches up on held work, including a download a manual Keep asked for. Nothing is linked or shared between the two rows, and a shared audio URL alone never qualifies. A link an earlier version made is undone from the row's context menu with **Unlink Episodes…**, which names what each episode keeps or loses first.

Association source-generation reads compare creation instants at the existing captured `Timestamp` precision: canonical ISO8601 values are decoded before comparison, so equivalent `Z` and `.000Z` forms match. Submillisecond changes that serialize to the same instant are indistinguishable in that capture. Changes to the recorded instant, raw episode ID, feed or membership still invalidate the association. Raw source dates and the capture/schema remain unchanged.

The per-show ad profile file that earlier builds wrote beside the library is left on disk. Nothing reads it.

The online labeller's validated reply decides the cut: the worker removes the sponsor and network-promo breaks the reply marks must-cut, plus the show's own promotion only when that feed's switch is on. A break that cannot be placed stays audible and is reported. The Swift report checker matches removed pieces within each keep-map gap, preserving even a positive one-millisecond kept interval between a pause and an ad. Rejections record fixed categories and counts.

- Off-the-list history is searchable and filterable in 25-row pages, preserves every record, and supports Restore. Feed capacity follows actual board/store readiness and exposes load errors with Retry.
- Larder rows use title, feed and length, then date, with one fixed action slot. Search starts empty, sync status has one footer, and global Download/Prepare controls count actionable rows without repeating group controls.

Manual playlists use saved membership order for Custom; calculated sorts do not write playlist or Larder order. History applies calculated ordering before page slicing through a scalar key scan; its default Custom order remains retirement order, with no schema change. Playlist rows tag each playlist Manual or Smart in a capsule beneath its name (Smart in the leaf, Manual in steel, so the tag never reads as part of the name), with the basket icon for a manual playlist and the colander for a smart one; the playlist page shows the same tag, and New Playlist offers New Smart Playlist.
- A podcast ending naturally continues with the first displayed eligible Ready episode, including an earlier unfinished row. Playlist and audiobook completion keep their own rules; a newer selection or Pause takes precedence.
- Downloads interrupted by quitting resume on relaunch, with at most three automatic attempts. Normal quit hides the window immediately while the existing bounded safe drain runs; failure or timeout restores the app and retry state. Explicit Cancel stays cancelled. Saved pending preparations for kept, downloaded episodes resume in their original order with their saved settings through the normal admission gate, respecting off-peak; malformed saved policies show a retry message.
- Alphabetical sorting on Mac and iPhone ignores a leading “The”, regardless of case, while keeping displayed titles intact. Share remains a normal action: with no usable page address, it shares `title - show` as text.
- Now Playing keeps Mark completed visible beside the transport, followed by a labelled native More menu (Restart, and Recover audio on a route fault). Share sits beside the episode title and Speed at the end of the Transcript/Notes row.
- Automatic playlists are built in a rule editor: stable numbered leaves combine with case-insensitive AND, OR and NOT and parentheses, for example `(1 OR 2 OR 3 OR 4 OR 5) AND 6 AND 7` over feed, Unplayed and Ready leaves, with a live matching count and titles as the formula changes and an inline validation problem that disables Save. - Mac and iOS startup use the approved larder shelf serpentine until actual local readiness. ## Git hooks

Install the repository-owned hooks once per clone:

```bash
make install-hooks
```

The `pre-commit` hook runs `make check-fast`: commits warn above 500 lines and fail
above 800 unless `.file-size-exceptions` lists the file with a reason. It checks staged
`.swift`, `.py`, and `.sh` files through `scripts/check_file_size.py`. `make validate` audits
the whole tree. The `pre-push` hook runs `make validate` and nothing else, so a push
never waits on the screen-seizing Mac UI run. The
installer is idempotent and sets the installing worktree's private
`core.hooksPath` to its `.githooks`, so installing in one linked worktree does
not change its siblings. A legacy shared `.githooks` setting is migrated only
when this is the sole worktree; with linked siblings, installation refuses
without changing Git configuration.

Run `make worktree-setup` once in each new linked worktree (`.claude/worktrees/*`,
a Codex worktree) before its gates. The runtime lock and the release wrappers
resolve the `speech-stack` and `apple_developer` projects beside the checkout, so
the target links each one beside the worktree to the main checkout's sibling,
syncs `Producer/Runtime/.venv` from `uv.lock`, and fails unless the speech
stack's GPU lock resolves to the shared `~/Documents/Projects/speech-stack/.state`.
It is idempotent and never replaces an existing entry that resolves elsewhere.

## Test tiers

1. **Commit:** `make check-fast` (lint, dead code, file size, scratch).
2. **Push:** `make validate`, the headless gate. It defers the `macos-ui-tests` leg and says so. A failing step never stops it: every step runs, and the run ends with one `validate.failed count=<n> steps=<names>` line.
3. **Install:** `make install` runs `scripts/verify-once-gate.sh check-ui` and refuses unless every `ui` leg of the adapter (`macos-ui-tests`) has a fresh verify-once receipt for the exact source of the tree being installed (any edit to a source input makes it stale). `WILTED_SKIP_UI_RECEIPT=1 make install` proceeds anyway and prints a warning. Use it only when the Mac UI leg cannot run. `make install-walkthrough` is the release walkthrough's install: it runs `make validate` and installs only if that passes, with no UI receipt, because the screen-seizing leg runs once after the walkthrough, on the final candidate.
4. **`make native-ui`** (or `app/verify-release-ui`): runs only the screen-seizing XCUITest leg through the same verify-once layer and records its receipt. Run it when away from the Mac.

**Verify once.** `scripts/verify-once-gate.sh` runs each `scripts/test-gate.sh` leg through the `apple_developer` receipt layer (legs declared as `gateLegs` in `.release/release-adapter.json`). A headless leg whose input files, toolchain and simulator runtime match a fresh receipt from any worktree is reused (`gate-leg <id>: reused`). A passing leg records a receipt in `<git common dir>/release-receipts/wilted-ios/<leg>/`. `make validate` and the release local gate (`app/release_local_gate.sh`) share these receipts. A narrowed run (`WILTED_GATE_LEGS` and the other selectors) never records one. `app/release-testflight` and `app/release-status` re-exec inside a tool-owned release checkout. Run `make worktree-setup` in a new worktree first (it also links the sibling `speech-stack`).

**Gate diagnostics.** Set `WILTED_GATE_DEBUG=1` to keep a private shell trace for each executed leg in `.logs/gate-debug/`. Each entry identifies the function, file and line. Expanded arguments stay out of the trace. Deadlines save a process tree, sample or failure note, and gate lock owner under `.logs/timeouts/`. Hosted legs report host state and refuse a pending Touch ID prompt or a concurrent hosted test with exit 2.

**Single-leg reruns.** `WILTED_GATE_LEGS=<comma list>` runs only the named legs of `scripts/test-gate.sh` (`xcodegen-reproducible`, `wiltedkit-tests`, `cloudsync-tests`, `playback-tests`, `wiltedproducer-tests`, `macos-unit-tests`, `ios-unit-tests`, `macos-ui-tests`, `ios-pixel-snapshot-tests`), for example `WILTED_GATE_LEGS=wiltedkit-tests bash scripts/test-gate.sh`. Unknown, empty or malformed names fail closed before any leg or simulator sweep runs. The summary still prints `native.complete`, lists the skipped legs on `native.leg.skipped` and `native.filtered` lines, and qualifies the pass line as `native.passed count=<n> filtered=<k>`. A filtered run is a diagnostic only: the verify-once layer records no receipt while the variable is set. `tests/test-native-gate-legs.sh` holds the meta-tests. The opt-in `watchos-build` leg hosts `WiltedWatchTests` on the persistent owned 45 mm watchOS simulator and shuts down only the simulator it booted. It writes four named shared-shipping-content PNGs under `.logs/watch-captures/<base-sha>/working-tree-*/`, labelled uncommitted when appropriate. Native containers and navigation are excluded. Speed and Sleep capture their full content at the actual 45 mm width and intrinsic height. This is not a full-screen, native-layout, or owner-acceptance receipt. The default gate is unchanged: name `WILTED_GATE_LEGS=watchos-build` in a separate gate run, not around `make validate`, whose phase-0 meta-tests would inherit the filter.

**Single-test reruns.** `make native-mac-unit TEST=<Class[/testMethod],...> [ITERATIONS=<n>]` runs one XCTest class or method of `WiltedMacTests`, N times, through the gate's own build, signing, lock and temp-ownership path. `make native-ios-unit` does the same for `WiltediOSTests` through the same simulator session and `apple-ui-test-lock` lane as the full iOS leg. For example `make native-mac-unit TEST=BulletinProducerTests/testAnUnchangedListIsNotRepublished ITERATIONS=5`. The targets set `WILTED_GATE_LEGS` to the one unit leg (which brings `xcodegen-reproducible`) plus `WILTED_MAC_UNIT_SELECTORS` and `WILTED_MAC_UNIT_ITERATIONS`, or `WILTED_IOS_UNIT_SELECTORS` and `WILTED_IOS_UNIT_ITERATIONS`. The variables also work directly with `bash scripts/test-gate.sh`. A selector is a comma list of `Class` or `Class/testMethod` (the gate adds the target name), and iterations are 1 to 100, passed to `xcodebuild -test-iterations`. An empty, malformed or metacharacter-bearing value fails closed before any leg starts. A Mac selector lowers that leg's test-count floor from 30 to 1, so a misspelled class fails as `native.zero-tests` instead of passing an empty run. Any of the four variables makes the run a filtered diagnostic: the summary adds `native.filtered.tests leg=<leg> selectors=<list|all> iterations=<n> receipt=never`, and the pass line always carries `filtered=<k>`, so no receipt can be minted from it. `make validate` unsets all four. Many iterations can outrun the leg's budget: the Mac unit leg builds and tests under one 1,200-second budget (`WILTED_XCODE_BUILD_AND_TEST_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`), while the iOS legs build first under the 1,800-second build budget and then test under the 600-second test budget (`WILTED_XCODE_TEST_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`; the pixel and journey leg defaults to 900 seconds because it relaunches the app for each of its 36 tests); raise the relevant one for a long run. To compare against a base commit that has these targets, run the same target in a detached scratch worktree (its first build is cold, because `.build` caches are per checkout):

```bash
base="$(mktemp -d "$TMPDIR/wilted-base.XXXXXX")"
git worktree add --detach "$base/wt" origin/main
make -C "$base/wt" native-mac-unit TEST=BulletinProducerTests/testAnUnchangedListIsNotRepublished ITERATIONS=5
git worktree remove "$base/wt" && rmdir "$base"
```

## Bounded tests and scratch

`make validate`, `make native-ui`, the gate scripts, and the shell test entrypoints use the repository's bounded runner (`scripts/run-bounded.py`) to clean up each command's observed child processes, including descendants that leave the original process group. Ordinary shell test entrypoints default to a 1,800-second work budget and individual build commands to 300 seconds. `WILTED_TEST_RUNNER_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` and `WILTED_TEST_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` override those budgets. Gate orchestrators instead wait with visible heartbeats and no default aggregate wall-clock deadline: dependency, cache-lock, simulator-readiness, and shared-destination waits do not consume a leg's work budget. Native builds default to 1,800 seconds and test work to 600 seconds, starting only after their required lock and readiness steps. Native Xcode legs use `build-for-testing` followed by `test-without-building`, retaining per-leg DerivedData. Shared test destinations serialize while independent builds proceed. `scripts/build-with-cache.py` reuses the checkout's shared `.build` caches, and lock waits are heartbeat-only unless a diagnostic lock timeout is explicitly set. The five test targets in `Producer/Runtime/Makefile` (`test`, `test-unit`, `test-integration`, `test-e2e`, and `test-tui`) run pytest through the same supervisor. Cancellation and timeout cleanup stop and reap owned descendants before releasing owned state. A timeout or cleanup failure is nonzero, and a deferred native UI leg is still a deferral, not a pass.

Create ephemeral Wilted scratch below the inherited `$TMPDIR` with a unique `mktemp -d` path, register cleanup before starting child work, stop/reap owned children, and preserve only cited evidence under `.logs/`. The native and Phase 0 gates remove their own scratch on normal exit and handled signals. Before starting, they sweep only root-level `wilted-*` entries at least 24 hours old with an explicit ownership marker, an inactive owner, and no holders, while historical unmarked or borrowed spec directories are preserved. The active-tree check `scripts/check-no-global-tmp.py` rejects hard-coded absolute global temporary-root references in operational scripts and documentation. Captain and worker procedures are recorded in the internal plan overlay and bounded-task template. Those templates require caller-shell cleanup and reuse of shared `.build` caches.

Final verification is retained in the local midnight receipt (`.logs/leak-repair-2026-09-28/midnight-final-receipt.txt`) and captain acceptance (`.logs/leak-repair-2026-09-28/captain-acceptance.json`). The obsolete zero-test result bundle was removed at the owner’s request on 2026-11-04.

## Scope

Larder is the one place kept episodes wait. The active podcast appears in Now Playing instead of a second Ready row. The durable queue retains its position for playback succession. Larder groups waiting episodes as Ready, Needs preparation, and Not downloaded, and shows a completed preparation summary only when a successful terminal journal matches the audio revision currently ready to play. A transcript by itself or a journal for an older revision is not presented as prepared. Ready-media reconciliation and export use the publication snapshot to omit unrelated transcript and preparation-history decoding while retaining exact prepared-media proof.

The active milestone turns the native SwiftUI Mac app into a daily driver. The existing article path stays intact. The milestone adds subscribed feeds, cancellable local episode downloads with visible progress, atomic enclosure imports, a durable Larder listening queue, continuous playback, direct seeking, persistent speed, and exact resume. Feeds, episodes, queue state, playback speed, and episode checkpoints remain local-only until Phase 3 Mac owner acceptance. The existing iOS listener and article CloudKit path remain unchanged while that Mac milestone is implemented and qualified.

Navigation is a destination switch on both platforms. The Mac sidebar lists Larder, Feeds, and Settings, in that order, followed by a Playlists section (manual playlists filled with Add to Playlist from a Larder row, and automatic playlists built from rules; playing one never changes the Larder order, W-INV-028); a toolbar button or Control-Command-S hides and shows it, and the window restores its selection, scroll positions, Larder filter and search, and drafts at launch (View > Clear Saved View resets them). Feeds is a one-decision inbox: Keep moves an episode to Larder and Skip moves it to the collapsed Off the list section. Refresh adds metadata only, exposes live progress and Cancel, and records the last successful refresh; undecided episodes never start download or preparation. Larder owns downloading, preparing, sorting, playback, and queue order. It is one list grouped Ready, Active work, Downloaded, then Not downloaded, holding episodes, articles and books, with a kind filter (All, Podcasts, Articles, Audiobooks); book rows and both players show the current chapter with previous and next steps. Adding goes through one Add sheet (toolbar Add, or File > Add… with Command-N): paste a podcast or article link, or type a show name to search Apple Podcasts (`itunes.apple.com/search` and `/lookup`, HTTPS only, no redirects, debounced), and each result offers one action, Subscribe or Add article, with shows you already follow marked. A feed with no audio episodes is offered as an article feed: Wilted refreshes it for new posts and offers them in the Feeds inbox, and a feed with neither audio nor posts is refused. Import book (or a file drop) imports audio files or a folder as one chaptered audiobook, identified by its content (W-INV-027), or turns EPUB or PDF text into a spoken book; archive and XML input is treated as untrusted (entity declarations refused, size and entry caps). Its independent **Group by** control offers Feed, Date, and Status, while **Sort by** offers Length, Age, Alphabetical, and Custom, with a saved direction for the ordered modes; each row shows its release date. The selected sort preference persists across playback, natural continuation, and app relaunch, while explicit manual drag and drop switches back to Custom order and draws the exact insertion gap. Each Larder row on Mac and iOS presents a second metadata line formatted as `Feed - duration`, presenting the feed title and total source duration, and a third line with the publication date alone, immediately below the title (the iPhone keeps the title to one line and, under a Feed group header, leaves the feed out of the second line); Mac Active work rows display live status in active metadata space (allowing up to two lines for stage and detail) without redundant empty-state article reminders. That Mac-owned preparation status is also carried on each entry as a bounded, versioned, display-only optional field: an absent, unsupported or malformed value is ignored and leaves the entry fully usable, and it never authorizes playback, media admission or a phone command; the update time is the last producer report, not an online heartbeat. The Mac publishes queued, active, failed and cancelled status only for kept exact backing entries, using the persisted ticket sequence and attempt count. Each progress update uses the existing debounced publisher; bound success clears the status only when the exact generation terminal matches the current Ready revision and current preparation outcome. Completed upgrade tickets with no bound journal defer to existing Ready/current outcome proof, while explicit bound revision mismatches retain status. Unconfirmed output retains display status; withdrawal clears it. Publication queries bounded generation journals and never decodes preparation history. Recognized stage text and bounded numeric counts are curated; raw worker errors and paths stay local. Phone companion status parity is deferred in TASKS.md. Manually starting an episode in the Larder preserves queue order and advances forward through subsequent eligible episodes, stopping at the end without wrapping backwards. Ready episodes play from top to bottom while non-ready rows are skipped. Now Playing is embedded in Larder and remains an always-visible bottom rail elsewhere; Transcript and Notes open inline in Larder and in the full-window player elsewhere. A selected Mac transcript shows a loading state while it is read; a read failure offers Retry, while confirmed absence remains unavailable. Settings exposes appearance plus feed refresh, processing schedule, transcript, and ad-removal defaults; admitted work keeps the policy snapshot it started with. Settings also offers a quiet chime, on by default, at each spot an advertisement was cut; it plays over the audio, so switching it needs no re-preparation. The iPhone library app is a Larder-only companion synced through CloudKit: it lists only episodes the Mac has prepared (in the shared play order, filtered and searched; its Sort and Group menus change only how the list is shown, playback still follows the play order, and it has no drag-reorder, the Mac keeps its own custom order), fetches audio from the Mac on request, opens an episode detail with artwork, notes and a follow-along transcript, plays with a persisted default speed, resumes from the newest of the Mac's and its own positions, hands playback off in both directions, publishes its own paused and periodic positions so the Mac adopts them (the Mac takes a phone position as its stored position only when it is newer than its own, for the exact revision it offers, and never for an episode it is playing or has finished), and offers Mark done and Remove from Larder as intents the Mac applies. Its Larder toolbar Add sheet searches Apple Podcasts on the phone and sends Subscribe and Add article to the Mac as `subscribe` and `addArticle` intents, showing "Sent to your Mac" until the Mac's outcome arrives; it sends nothing when the Mac does not advertise the action in `supportedIntentActions` and says to update Wilted on the Mac instead. Its Settings sheet (gear) shows appearance, playback, storage, sync status (including an iCloud rate-limit pause) and this phone's own lifetime statistics; the Mac stays the only writer of library state. A dated Mac verification report is required before a major build or rollup deployment, while local debug installations use their own gate evidence; neither claims owner acceptance.

**Mac Settings** reopens on the last tab you used. Listening contains General and Preparing. Feeds & Reports contains New episodes and Reports. Ads & Providers contains Ads and Providers. Library & Sync contains Devices and About.

In Providers, **Check** tests one provider and **Check all** tests all seven. A key provider with a saved key receives a short fixed message with a one-token output limit. A provider without a saved key receives no request.

Feeds Skip is reversible. Skipped and retired episodes remain stored and appear under Feeds' collapsed **Off the list** section, where Restore clears the stored removal without a feed fetch or rematch. Restore reuses retained verified audio after manual completion. Episodes whose audio was deleted require download again. Larder has no Skip decision: a started, unfinished row offers **Mark completed** (on the iPhone also any row whose audio is on the phone, behind a confirmation); every row offers **Remove from Larder**, which uses the same retained-media manual completion as Mark completed, preserving downloads, prepared cuts, transcripts, and measured listening history. On the Mac a toast pinned over the bottom of the window says what was marked completed and offers **Undo** for 5 seconds, on every destination and wherever the list is scrolled; Edit > Undo (Command-Z) takes back the same thing. The removal is saved at once and Undo reverses it, so quitting inside the 5 seconds leaves the episode removed, with Restore under Feeds' Off the list as the way back. A second removal is stacked on the first, newest undone first. The stored undo itself lasts 10 seconds, and the toast waits that long while the pointer rests on it or VoiceOver is running. Undo restores prior queue placement and decision provenance without starting playback. **Played** means a durable completion record exists. Pressing Next at 95% or more of a loaded podcast's live playhead completes and retires it after the next playable episode opens; below 95%, it keeps the outgoing position resumable. Previous selects the nearest earlier playable queued episode. Store version 13 owns one `removalKind` column that says an episode is retired, dismissed, or neither, while downloaded podcast revision identity includes both the episode and verified content hash so identical bytes from different episodes cannot collide. Existing content-hash-only podcast revisions remain readable and are reused only for the same item and exact hash. Article and TTS revision identity is unchanged.

Natural completion and skipping an episode from Feeds retire it from Larder into Feeds' Off the list section. Retirement keeps the episode row, decisions, listening history and preparation journal, and deletes its audio: the revision, download and artwork records go, and once that save commits so do their files (W-INV-025, records equal audio). Manual Mark completed and Remove from Larder instead apply retained-media completion to started or unstarted episodes, keeping audio, transcripts and revisions on disk so Undo restores them offline. A file another surviving episode still names stays. Each launch also runs a reclaim step (`Reclaiming storage`) that deletes media files no record names and clears records whose file is missing; re-downloading an episode deletes the copy it replaces. Feeds keeps the history, so a deleted episode can be downloaded again. On launch, the retirement sweep also repairs completed episodes left active by an earlier build. A revisionless legacy completion retires when its current ready audio predates completion. A later re-download remains active. A store still carrying the pre-V13 separate dismissal-tombstone table folds each tombstone onto its episode row (recreating the row as a placeholder if it was already gone) through the same idempotent post-open reconciliation that repairs work tickets, run once every launch rather than once per schema bump. Manual-completion recovery replays admission only for feeds named by completion receipts; a receipt-free startup does not trigger unrelated admission.

Preparing a downloaded episode is automatic and runs in stages: transcript stage first (the feed's published transcript, else local speech-to-text aligned to the audio in worker process `Producer/Workers/wilted_pipeline.py`), then labelling in Swift, then the worker cuts the labelled breaks and remaps cues onto the cut audio so the transcript follows playback. A published transcript is used only when it describes the file that was actually downloaded: hosts insert advertising at request time, so the same episode is served at different lengths, and timing taken from a shorter rendering drifts the reading position and points the cut at conversation. The worker compares the transcript's last cue against the audio's probed duration, records the measurement on every run, and falls through to speech-to-text when the audio is longer by more than 45 seconds or half a percent. That comparison governs transcript-only preparation. It decides nothing on a removal run: when ad removal is asked for, the publisher's transcript is not fetched or parsed at all, and one aligned `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` pass is both the labeller's input and the transcript that is displayed. A destructive cut requires that aligned pass and nothing else. A request that forbids local speech-to-text while asking for ad removal is refused before any model work, and timing that is not finite, not in order, or does not fit the downloaded audio within a declared three-second tail is refused rather than repaired, because quietly repairing it would aim real cuts using timing nothing has vouched for. The aligned speech-to-text result is cached atomically by immutable audio revision, validated before reuse, refreshed on access, and bounded to 32 entries, so a retry does not transcribe unchanged audio again. The worker is Python because the preparation runtime is project-owned under `Producer/Runtime/src` and `Producer/Workers`; the generated interpreter lives under `Producer/Runtime/.venv`, and no archive path is part of the active runtime; `WILTED_PIPELINE_PYTHON`, `WILTED_PIPELINE_WORKER`, `WILTED_PIPELINE_PYTHONPATH`, `WILTED_PIPELINE_TIMEOUT_S`, and `WILTED_PIPELINE_TOOL_PATH` override the locations. The local language model (repaired Gemma GGUF under `~/models/gemma-4-repaired`) is used for the transcript summary; cut tools (`ffmpeg`/`ffprobe` reachable from the worker's PATH, extended with `/opt/homebrew/bin` and `/usr/local/bin`) are checked before speech-to-text. The worker acquires the canonical GPU lock with live progress, verifies zero daemon residency and no other admitted work while holding it, releases and drains before retrying when needed, and retains the lock through model execution. Waiting for that lock and talking to the daemon behind it have separate budgets: waiting on a peer that legitimately holds the GPU is allowed to take as long as that peer's work, while each daemon call after the lock is held keeps a ten-second bound so a daemon that has stopped answering still fails visibly. The app also admits one preparation at a time and queues the rest, because concurrent runs do not share the GPU, they evict each other's resident speech models and retry; a waiting episode reads Queued rather than Preparing. On the labelled path, ads are cut only on a validated labelling reply from the online provider. The worker refuses a request for ad removal without a labelling stage with the error "local ad finding was removed". A show that is not sent keeps its ads: the original audio is kept whole and the episode carries a durable warning `original audio includes ads: <reason sentence>`. Genuine missing or invalid audio, render failures, and invalid proof remain typed failures that preserve the previous audio. Outcomes are `disabled`, `noAds`, `cut` and `uncertain` (the whole original is kept with a warning). A successful cut requires a safe nonempty keep map and a rendered duration within 0.35 seconds of the map's prediction. The cut is re-encoded accurately rather than stream-copied, and what the run reports as removed is the exact complement of the map the renderer was given. A completed terminal journal entry preserves validated, bounded removed and kept intervals, allowing Prep to show every original cut at its prepared-file seam after relaunch. Prep keeps each run's narrative above one action row and exposes its selectable monospaced event log in a bounded scroll region. Retry always starts work or reports why the source is unavailable. Cancellation finishes only after cleanup. Redoing a good preparation ("Prepare this copy again") and fetching the publisher's file again ("Download again, then prepare") are both in the Larder row's … menu on any downloaded episode, under a line saying that preparing writes the cut audio over the download. That is why the fetch exists: a run that cut the wrong seconds has consumed the only copy of its own source, so re-preparing alone cannot undo it, and the labels name which file each route starts from. A run the journal still calls live when the app launches was interrupted with its process, since every run belongs to the process that started it. Bootstrap closes it as failed with the reason, and Prep offers the retry.

The feed's show notes are stored with each episode (store version 9; `description`, `content:encoded`, or `itunes:summary`, whichever is longest, reduced from HTML to plain text with each link's address kept in parentheses, capped at 32 KiB) and shown three ways: the Larder row leads with the notes' opening paragraph instead of the author, the row's title opens an episode information popover whose **Notes** are shown by default, and Now Playing has a Notes pane beside Transcript. That popover also offers a **Transcript** tab with the episode's already-saved transcript text, including a retired or already-played episode while its text is retained, a neutral line when nothing is saved, and a Retry action when the read fails. The same episode-name popover works from Larder, Feeds, History (Off the list and feed details), the rules preview, Playlists, and the player pane and rail, while articles keep ordinary titles. Opening it prepares no transcript and leaves Now Playing's transcript and playback independent, and dismissing or unsubscribing still deletes transcripts as before. iPhone parity is follow-up. Every address is clickable in both readings. The title underlines itself under the pointer rather than colouring every row in the list, and a row whose feed published no notes is plain text with nothing to press. They also ride in the worker request as `episodeNotes`, where they become a glossary: the hosts, guests, products, and sponsor sites the notes spell out are applied to the transcript after the cut, exact lower-case hits taking the notes' casing and near-misses ("sama boul samad", "adaptive security dot com") taking the spelling when the letters are at least 80% alike and the target is not an ordinary word. One `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` pass supplies aligned cues for both ad finding and playback timing on every removal run, and on a transcript-only run whose feed published nothing usable. It writes punctuated, capitalized sentences, so the glossary's exact hits mostly confirm casing the model already chose; a segment it closes with no duration (Rogan #2565 emitted 2616.48-2616.48 "are.") is dropped before anything reads it, because the aligned cache refuses the whole pass for one. It replaced `parakeet-tdt-1.1b` under engine podcast-preparation-v13 (2026-10-27): over five fresh episodes it cut about 117 s of programme where 1.1b cut about 445 s, and left about 97 s of advertising where 1.1b left about 90 s; already prepared episodes keep their cuts. Segments are put in time order before anything reads them: transcription runs in 120-second GPU windows with 15 seconds of overlap, and stitching them emits a few segments that start before the one ahead of them (four in 1028 on a two-hour episode, every one at a window seam). The display cues never showed it because they are sorted on projection, and the aligned cache refused to save every episode it was ever offered.

Larder episode rows show active and failed preparation states, plus a completed `Ready`, transcript, and ad-removal summary only when the successful terminal journal matches the audio revision currently ready to play. Prep retains the full recorded run.

The iPhone Larder also shows the Mac’s current queued, active, failed and cancelled preparation reports before audio is available. These read-only reports include stage-local progress and a last-reported time for VoiceOver. They do not grant downloads, playback, preparation or cancellation. Available and Downloaded retain their media meaning. Associated copies retain separate reports in raw-member order, with other-copy reports identified explicitly. Withdrawal or removal hides retained reports.

**Untrusted XML.** Every podcast feed, article payload, and EPUB XML file passes one linear, bounded classifier (`UntrustedXML`) before `XMLParser` exists. It decodes the payload once, refuses malformed, unsupported, or self-contradicting encodings with a typed reason, then walks the decoded bytes once: comments end at the first `-->` and processing instructions at the first `?>`, regardless of quotes; quotes hide `>` and `[` only inside tags and DOCTYPE literals; an `<!ENTITY` declaration counts only in a markup-declaration position and a DTD internal subset only outside a DOCTYPE literal. Supported encodings are UTF-8, UTF-16 (BE or LE, from a BOM or the XML byte signature), UTF-32 (BE or LE), ISO-8859-1, and windows-1252; every other spelling, an encoding that contradicts the BOM or signature, and truncated or undefined byte sequences (including UTF-7's shifted declarations, which the old scanners missed) are refused before parsing, while the original bytes are handed to `XMLParser` unchanged. Podcast feeds refuse every DOCTYPE; article feeds and EPUB XML keep accepting a bare DOCTYPE that carries only a public or system identifier. Both no-resolution controls are set (`shouldResolveExternalEntities = false` and `externalEntityResolvingPolicy = .never`), with the existing consumer-specific resolver behavior retained. Owned-canary tests check returned text and refusal outcomes; they do not trace resource I/O. The existing caps are unchanged and stay distinct: feed and article payloads are bounded by `PodcastFeedClient.maximumFeedBytes` (16 MiB) in the HTTPS-only loader, EPUB XML files by `BookExtractionLimits.maxXMLBytes` (8 MB), and EPUB archives by the 5,000-entry and 500 MB unpacked caps plus the 200,000-node and 200-deep XML node ceiling.

The Larder projection (`loadLibrary`) reads one full `podcastLibrarySnapshot()` per refresh rather than assembling itself from a store call per article and per episode. Its indexed read includes articles, episodes, feeds, subscriptions, downloads, ready revisions, playback, transcripts, preparation outcomes, listening state, retirement, and full preparation history. The library publisher uses the snapshot's publication profile, which omits transcript bodies and preparation-run history; download-byte updates therefore do not read those display-only payloads just to publish library state. Preparation run evidence remains uncapped in the full Larder snapshot. Prep's own `preparationRuns()` still caps its display list at 200 recent runs.

It was reference-only and non-runnable by then: absolute paths, legacy runtime bindings, an SQLite database, a shell alias, and retired launchd jobs. On 2026-08-17 the owner approved transferring the existing GitHub repository to this fresh project; the old Git history was not migrated, and the parts never ported (its Phase-0 spikes, plans, reports, and verification records) are gone with it. ## Sync cadence

Each device has one 30 s sync tick (`WiltedKit` `SyncTick`, `SyncCadence.tickInterval`). Every periodic read, publish, checkpoint and pending-decision check batches into its rounds and nothing runs faster. A user action sends its one write at once, a confirming read waits for the next round, a rate limit holds the whole tick for the server's Retry-After, and pull to refresh runs a round now (or shows the retry state while limited). The rule, the per-round contents, the request-rate audit and `scripts/sync-ops-rate.sh` (operations per minute from the unified log) are in `docs/2026-10-01-sync-cadence.md`; the tests that pin it are `SyncTickTests`, `LibraryPollRequestCountTests`, `WiltedMacSyncRoundTests` and `LibraryPhoneSyncTickTests`.

Playback progress polling queries observed device-entry pairs and explicit track seeds. Passive media offers do not multiply progress queries across devices. Active now-playing records and known-pair positions use the normal 30 s cadence, subject to backoff; newly discovered active playback fetches progress in the same round. Unseen inactive progress-only pairs are discovered through the periodic whole-zone scan (normally up to 20 minutes on Mac with known peers) before joining polling. This reduces speculative requests. It does not establish that live CloudKit throttling is resolved.

## Installing and the library publisher

`make install` (`scripts/install-mac-app.sh`) builds the **Development** configuration and replaces the copy in `/Applications`, quitting a running copy first. Development compiles `WILTED_CLOUDKIT_LIVE` and carries the iCloud/CloudKit and development-push entitlements, so the installed app runs the library publisher against the Development CloudKit environment by default. It signs with the Apple Development identity and the provisioning profile already cached on the Mac, never passes `-allowProvisioningUpdates` and never contacts the developer portal. A missing or expired cached profile fails the build, and refreshing it is an attended step in Xcode. Debug (unit tests) has no live transport and Release is the separate Developer ID build.

`WILTED_LIBRARY_SYNC` picks the sync engine for a launch (`WiltedMacLibraryRuntimeSelection`): unset selects the library publisher in a live Development or Release build; `1` forces it on; any other non-empty value (for example `0`) forces it off (`.none`). There is no legacy engine to return to. Test hosts, non-live builds and UI fixtures run with no sync engine (`.none`).

**Account binding.** The publisher is bound to one iCloud account owner and sends nothing until the current account is that owner (W-INV-021). An empty library binds its first owner without review. A library that has data but no binding, a sign-out, or a different account holds sending. Settings then shows why and offers a review, and approving it ("Use reviewed account") resumes sending for the reviewed account. Status text and logs carry no account identifier.

**Settings sync status.** The Sync card shows one status line for the library publisher, resolved from the account binding, the shared rate-limit throttle and local activity: waiting, working, pending, sent, idle, or a send or check failure, with a plain reason for each account hold. Times are this Mac's own send and read times; nothing on the card claims the iPhone has fetched or converged. **Sync now** runs one round, joins a round already running instead of starting another, and is not offered while an account hold or throttle applies. Relaunch account checks use the same shared throttle and preserve iCloud's requested wait. An unverified saved owner cannot admit a refresh. This is source- and fixture-tested only. The installed app and a live CloudKit account have not been exercised.

**Activity.** Clicking the sidebar's sync line opens a popover with one line for each thing the Mac does in the background: library sync, device changes read, audio uploaded ahead of time to iCloud, iPhone audio requests, the iCloud request rate, direct downloads, preparation and automation. It shows state only and has no controls.

**Linked devices.** In Mac Settings > Library & Sync > Devices, each row shows a local name or device kind and the last announcement. Devices learned only from requests or playback show “Unknown”. The row menu lets you rename a device on this Mac or confirm removal of its link. An old directory entry cannot relink a removed device after relaunch. A newer announcement can, with direct downloads still stopped.

**Media delivery.** The iPhone asks for a prepared episode's audio and the Mac answers it in one of two ways. With **Download from your Mac over Wi-Fi** on (iPhone Settings, asked once at onboarding, on by default) and the Mac nearby, the phone finds the Mac over Bonjour (`_wilted-media._tcp`) and fetches the file straight from it over TLS 1.2 with a key only the owner's devices derive (W-INV-041); a cut transfer resumes from the bytes on disk, a download can finish in the background, and any failure falls back to iCloud. The Mac also uploads the next 10 prepared Larder entries that are not finished and not yet on a device to iCloud ahead of time, one at a time, so Get audio for those needs no Mac; other entries upload when asked. The Mac's **Direct downloads from this Mac** switch turns its server off, and each linked iPhone can be stopped on its own. Through iCloud, a request is saved on the phone and the Mac upload queue is saved on the Mac, so both survive a relaunch, and a throttle makes the row wait with a time instead of failing; Cancel is always offered. Either way the file is verified against the Mac's offer (byte count and SHA-256) before it plays (W-INV-007). Settings on both apps shows a diagnostics line with CloudKit operations per kind. Code: `WiltedKit/Sources/WiltedLibrary/LAN/`, `WiltedMac/Sync/WiltedMacLANMediaServer.swift`, `WiltedMac/Sync/WiltedMacMediaUploadQueue.swift`, `WiltediOS/Library/LibraryDirectMedia.swift`, `WiltediOS/Library/LibraryAppModel+MediaJob.swift`. Transport findings: `docs/lan-transport-spike-2026-11-02.md`.

**Quit.** A normal quit (`WiltedMacTerminationCoordinator`) pauses, stops admitting work and writes the playhead, lifetime totals and in-flight download bytes within 10 s. If that fails or times out the quit is cancelled, the app stays open and offers a retry. Network work then gets only the remaining budget and a bounded join: pending cloud sends are already durable locally, so a slow cloud step never blocks or cancels a quit (W-INV-019). A forced exit or crash skips the drain and loses at most about 10 s of listening time and 1 s of download bytes.

**Store V14 (lifetime statistics).** The first launch on a build with V14 migrates the library store, adding the lifetime ledger and the played-minute, downloaded-byte and manual-skip counters. Before migrating, the store checkpoints its WAL, rehearses on a clone, verifies row counts, and keeps a backup next to the store (`<store>.v<N>-backup-<UUID>/`) that is never deleted automatically. A populated table that would be lost fails the migration and restores the original. A store newer than the build is refused untouched, so downgrading needs the pre-migration backup and loses events recorded since (W-INV-020). Procedure, restore steps and evidence: [`docs/statistics-migration-recovery.md`](docs/statistics-migration-recovery.md). Only fixture stores have been migrated.

**Playback and deletion.** Mac playback commands go through one owner where the newest explicit command wins and Play/Pause is acknowledged before the first await (W-INV-017). A pause after an episode ends holds the queue advance (W-INV-018). While moving to the next episode after you mark the current one completed, Now Playing shows the existing Wilted loading animation and “Loading next episode…”; it honors Reduce Motion and clears if the advance is cancelled or fails. The iPhone uses its own location. It receives headlines and bulletin settings from the Mac during playback. Add news feeds and check their News setting on the Mac. **Play a test bulletin** speaks a short sample without changing the library or playback position.

**Feed automation.** Each feed has four automation controls on the Mac, on its Show settings page, available from Feeds, a Larder row or Settings > Ads & Providers > Ads > All shows: **Auto keep**, **Auto download**, **Auto prepare** (each On, Off or Same as default) and **Kept limit** (Same as default or a count of 1 or more). ## Labelled ad cuts

On an episode sent for labelling, one validated whole-episode labelling reply is permission to cut the sponsor and network-promo breaks it labels must-cut (charter W-INV-042). No audio review or second model confirms the cut. The Python worker removes the sponsor and network-promo breaks the reply marks must-cut, plus the show's own promotion only when that feed's switch is on (`removeSelfPromotion`). A break that cannot be placed stays audible and is reported. A reply whose cue digest does not match the transcript, or a cut over the share ceiling, keeps the whole original audio and records a durable warning. The worker refuses a request for ad removal without a labelling stage with the error "local ad finding was removed".

Prep selects its newest 200 run identities before decoding their full event details. Selected request histories are read in pages. Raw metadata scans remain where the schema has no attempt index. Current status reads use exact terminal IDs and paged attempt reads, preserving decoded timestamp ordering and Ready-generation proof. Overlapping Prep refreshes coalesce, reject superseded store results, and join shutdown cleanup. Larder snapshots and startup recovery retain uncapped history. A playing revision's cut timeline still uses the recent 64-row scan, then an exact current-outcome lookup and the bounded Ready-generation proof when newer failures bury its success. These reads do not prune history or change the schema. Storage indexing and retention remain separate decisions.

A show that is not sent for ad finding keeps its ads: the original audio is kept whole and the episode carries a durable warning `original audio includes ads: <reason sentence>`. The fixed reasons are online ad finding off, no provider key saved, and show not confirmed. A confirmed show at the daily request limit is not cut locally: its episode waits for the next local midnight when that falls inside the wait limit, and otherwise keeps the whole original audio with a warning that the daily limit was reached.

The cut stage maps labelled break times onto audio keep intervals, re-encoding accurately rather than stream-copying, and trims silence on the cut audio. An unresolved break or cut failure keeps the whole original audio playable. Offline evaluation tools retain recorded-mode corpus scoring (`ad_corpus.py` recorded mode, `ad_corpus_acquire.py`, `ad_corpus_recorded.py`), `ad_feedback_export.py`, the `ad_gold_*.py` tools, and `ad_proximity_eval.py`.

## Silence trimming

When ad removal is on, preparation also shortens long pauses. `wilted_worker/silence_trim.py` runs one ffmpeg `silencedetect` pass over the original audio (noise floor -50 dB, pauses of 0.25 s or more; the floor is justified in `constants.py`), reporting a heartbeat, and subtracts the excess from the keep map: a pause longer than 0.5 s keeps only its first and last 0.25 s (a pause of 0.5 s or less is left alone), and silence at the very start or end of the episode goes entirely. Pauses are measured on the ad-cut clock, so a pause that an ad cut joins together is trimmed once, and the keep map stays within 500 intervals by trimming the longest pauses first.

Trimmed pauses are never ads. The ad spans remain the complement of the ad-only keep map. The pauses travel in the result's separate `silenceTrims` list and never enter `adSegments`, `removedSeconds`, the ad lifetime statistic or the timeline's removed list. An episode whose only change is shorter pauses reports `noAds` with changed audio, and the Swift decoder accepts that only with a non-empty `silenceTrims` that, together with the ads, exactly fills every gap in the keep map. A saved position inside a trimmed pause resumes where the speech does. Disabled and uncertain results are never trimmed (W-INV-013). A detection failure keeps the pauses, and a failed silence-only render keeps the original audio. Both report an `audio.silence.skipped` warning and the episode is still prepared. The worker provenance is podcast-preparation-v12. Already prepared revisions are not re-processed.

## Online ad labeller

Off by default. When the owner saves a provider key in Mac Settings > Ads & Providers > Providers, turns the switch on under Ads and confirms the eligible shows in the preview, each newly prepared episode of a confirmed show is labelled by an online model (DeepSeek v4.1 Flash at the `low` level through the OpenCode Go endpoint): one streamed request whose body holds only the model name, the level, the streaming options and the prompt (the show title and the timed transcript). For those episodes the labeller's reply decides the cut (charter W-INV-042): the worker transcribes, the app asks once about the whole transcript, and the worker removes the sponsor and network-promo breaks the reply marks must-cut, plus the show's own promotion only when that feed's switch is on. A break that cannot be placed stays audible and is reported. A reply whose cue digest does not match the transcript, or a cut over the share ceiling, keeps the whole original. Shows that are not sent for ad finding keep their ads.

**Show settings and consent.** The count in Settings > Ads & Providers > Ads includes subscribed podcasts with saved permission, even when the overall switch is off. A show the owner turned off stays off. Reset all revokes a show’s permission before resetting its other choices. Resetting **Remove ads** also revokes permission if returning to the default would turn ad removal back on. The next preparation therefore stays local until the owner allows ad finding for the show again.

**What leaves the Mac.** For a show sent to the online labeller, each labelling request goes to the OpenCode Go endpoint and holds only the model name, the level, the streaming options and the prompt: the publisher's show title and the timed transcript. No audio, feed address, episode title or owner's own name for the show is sent, and a show that is not sent sends nothing. To stop it, turn the switch off in Mac Settings > Ads & Providers > Ads, or turn off **Send this show for ad finding** or set **Remove ads** to **Off (Custom)** in the show's settings.

- Routing is `RemoteLabellingPolicy`: an episode is sent for labelling only when the switch is on, a key is saved and the show is turned on for ad finding; a show that is not sent keeps its ads and its episodes carry a warning that says why; a show with **Remove ads** set to **Off (Custom)** or an episode prepared with ad removal off sends nothing, is never cut and carries no warning. A show added later is not sent, and keeps its ads, until it is turned on in its settings.
- `LabellerClient` refuses every redirect, caps the reply at 8 MB and never logs the key. `LabellerRequestCounter` counts every HTTP request against the daily cap (60).
- Second route, no key used: `defaults write com.zerodelta.wilted.mac wilted.adLabeller.useOpenCodeCLI -bool true` and a relaunch make the app send each request through the signed-in OpenCode command-line tool (`OpenCodeCLILabellerSender`, `/opt/homebrew/bin/opencode run --standalone --format json`, prompt on standard input) instead of `LabellerClient`. The tool runs with a private home in the temporary directory, so none of the owner's global agent instructions, skills or settings travel with the transcript; its agent file denies every permission and the model is offered no shell, file or web tool; the session it stores is deleted after each request (a run killed before the tool prints its session ID, or a failed delete, leaves that session in the tool's local store and logs a warning under category `AdLabellerCLI`). Its error output is discarded. If the preference is on but the tool is missing, the app falls back to the key route without saying so. `transport` in the records shows which ran. The tool adds about 700 tokens of its own (model name, date, working directory). The routing policy, the daily cap and the saved-key requirement are unchanged (a key must still be saved for the switch to turn on). Each record and attempt line carries `transport` (`direct` or `opencode-cli`) and the report counts them apart, because the two routes are not the same measurement.
- `LabelledAcceptanceReplayTests` (Producer package) replays saved labelling replies through the reply check and the break objects the cut stage receives, and fails when they differ from the saved result. It runs only when `WILTED_LABELLED_ACCEPTANCE_DIR` names a folder of `cases/<id>/` (`cues.json`, `case.json`, `reply.txt`); with `WILTED_LABELLED_ACCEPTANCE_LIVE=1` it also sends one live request for each case that has no saved reply. Transcripts and replies are third-party content and stay outside the repository. On the owner's Mac the folder is `.logs/delivery/phase2-acceptance`, with `prepare.py` and `score.py` beside it.
- Waits (`LabellerRetryPolicy`): a rate limit, an unreachable provider or a provider usage limit parks the episode's work ticket (`deferRunningWorkTicket`) and asks again after 2, 5, 15 and 30 minutes, then hourly, until two hours have passed since the first wait (`wilted.adLabeller.waitLimitSeconds`). At the daily cap the episode waits for the next local midnight when that falls inside the limit. A rejected key, an unavailable model or an unusable answer stops at once. A stopped run keeps the whole original, with the warning `original audio includes ads: <reason>`. After three episodes run out the wait limit, later provider waits stop at once until any provider response arrives. While an episode waits, its verified transcript cues are kept in `media/preparation/labeller-cues/` (owner-only file, deleted on any ending but a wait) so the next attempt skips transcription. Prepare Again never waits.
- Staleness: each attempt journals the policy digest it ran under. A labelled outcome's digest folds the route, model, level and prompt version, so an episode on a remotely routed show is offered for Prepare Again, and its info popover says "Prepared with an earlier ad-finding setting.", when the digest in force differs from the recorded one (`LabelledPolicyDigestInForce`). The daily cap is ignored for this, and rows with no recorded digest are judged by engine version only.
- The library store is schema 22: a work ticket records when it was first parked and how many times (one-way migration).

An iPhone build that predates the state decodes no status for that entry. The Mac row, the info popover (`WiltedMacPreparationStamp`: wait sentence, the Mac-only not-playable note, and `Ads left in: <reason>` for a run that stopped) and the Ad finding **Provider** row (shown only while `providerPausedSince` is stored) use the same wording.

- UX changes are designed and reviewed in the native app itself. Do not build HTML prototypes or mockups as a gate before native work.
- Keep all Wilted-owned project files self-contained under this directory. The preparation runtime may use only its documented shared `~/Documents/Projects/speech-stack` prerequisite. The reference-only archive is not an active dependency.
- Secrets belong in BWS and never in committed files.
- UI code does not write producer state directly. Shared domain contracts own identity, transfer, and playback reconciliation.
- Native UI follows the canonical Zero Delta structure with Wilted's restrained lettuce identity. Use literal navigation labels and reserve semantic colors for state.
- Where an SF Symbol clearly stands for a one- or two-word status or action (play, skip, remove, download, retry, played, in progress), the icon is the default: icon-only, with the word kept as its tooltip (`.help`) and in its accessibility label. Phrases, counts, and anything without an unambiguous symbol stay text.

Record mode keeps a Mac baseline that still matches 99% of its pixels. `WILTED_FORCE_RECORD_SNAPSHOTS=1` rewrites the named methods' baselines anyway. The Mac recorder continues to copy its PNGs back so `git status` shows exactly what moved. The iOS recorder makes fresh requested PNGs and publishes the complete requested set atomically only after every requested output succeeds. It preserves unrequested baselines and all prior bytes on recording failure, with failed-case diagnostics. The Mac daily-driver walkthrough is captured with `scripts/record-walkthrough-frames.sh`, built into an HTML report by `scripts/build-mac-walkthrough.py`, and audited by `scripts/audit-walkthrough.sh`.
- Update `HISTORY.md` alongside meaningful changes and keep pending work in `TASKS.md`.
- `LocalLibraryStore`'s SwiftData schema is versioned (`LocalLibrarySchemaVersion`, currently V22, with V19 adding durable manual-completion receipts, V20 adding confirmed same-feed association records, V21 dropping the retired sync engine's tombstones, repository state and unused sync columns and V22 adding the work ticket's first-deferral time and deferral count; see "Installing and the library publisher" below for the historical V14 statistics migration) and every version-bumping change adds a brand-new frozen model class rather than mutating a shipped one, migrating through `LocalLibraryMigrationPlan`'s lightweight stages. Add a rule only when a specific pipeline change actually made prior output wrong; an unrelated refactor, comment, or logging change should never force a redownload or re-preparation. `semanticVersion` is bumped by hand when preparation behavior changes; there are no checked-in source-hash pins to update. `WiltedMacModel` takes its rule table via an `invalidationRules` init parameter defaulting to the production table, so tests can inject a scenario-specific rule without changing what ships. A rule's `.forceRedownload` consequence is admitted one at a time through a detached bootstrap-recovery task (not inline in `performStoreBootstrap()`), so a library-wide migration with many stale episodes cannot open N simultaneous transfers and cannot hold the app in a non-ready state for the whole recovery burst; `waitForPodcastOperations()` is the test seam that waits for that task to drain.
- The 0.2.8 ad-recovery worker records `podcast-preparation-v4` with refreshed worker and Swift source hashes. Existing prepared audio remains eligible because the production invalidation-rule table is empty; a listener who wants a new preparation on a particular episode must explicitly request it, redownloading first if the current audio was cut.
- `HISTORY.md` and `TASKS.md` are local operational documents and are ignored by Git.
- Every podcast download, podcast preparation, and article preparation request is durable: `LocalLibraryStore` keeps one `WorkTicket` row per `(kind, subjectID)`, moving through `pending`/`deferred` (admitted, not yet running) → `running` → a terminal state (`succeeded`/`failed`/`cancelled`). `WorkTicketState.canTransition(to:)` only allows forward movement within one request sequence. `readmitWorkTicket` atomically replaces a settled attempt’s policy and stale failure/run fields for a newer sequence, while late older writes are ignored and duplicate running writes count only once. `applyWorkTicketTransition` does the whole find/validate/mutate/save as one actor-isolated call with no `await` in the middle. The actor itself is what serializes two Tasks racing to transition the same subject, given a conflicting insert on `@Attribute(.unique)` measurably throws on neither side and would otherwise lose a write silently. An article's `subjectID` is `ItemID.derive(from:)` the pasted draft URL (pure and networkless, so it is stable at request time), and its `resolvedItemID` is stamped once extraction returns the canonical URL, which a draft link and the canonical article it redirects to can disagree on. `reconcileWorkTickets(in:)` runs on every launch, before the library loads: it imports preferences-held off-peak deferrals and orphaned podcast downloads as tickets, closes any ticket left `running` by a process that is not this one (retried as `pending`, or failed once its attempt budget is spent), collapses an article-preparation duplicate that another draft URL's ticket already resolved to the same `resolvedItemID` (keeping the most recently requested, cancelling the rest), and prunes terminal tickets older than 30 days while always keeping at least the newest 50 per kind. A ticket-drain ticker (`startTicketDrainTicker`, distinct from `startAutomationTicker`) evaluates admission for whatever non-terminal tickets exist on a fixed interval and, unlike the open-window tick, keeps running while the window is hidden (`checkpointForBackground` never stops it, only actual termination does) because an off-peak window opening behind a hidden window used to go unnoticed until the window came back.

## Build cache and scratch

Run SwiftPM and Xcode builds through `python3 scripts/build-with-cache.py run <swiftpm|xcode> <label> -- <command>`. Both kinds clear cached File Provider metadata before building (SwiftPM the whole package cache, Xcode only the cache's `Build` tree, so the shared installer cache never touches another leg's DerivedData) and, on a detritus signing failure, clear again and retry once within the shared work budget, reporting `native.xattr.retry`. Raw `swift build`, `swift test` or `xcodebuild` calls outside the helper get no such protection. The helper reuses stable package caches under `.build/swiftpm/<label>` and one `.build/xcode` DerivedData in this worktree, serializes builds sharing a cache, and reports waits. Use `path` to inspect a cache without building. Temporary diagnostics use a marked `mktemp -d` directory under `$TMPDIR` and are removed at exit; cited evidence and tool runtime logs live in gitignored `.logs/`, and the Mac app's own warning log lives in its state directory (`WiltedMac/WiltedMacLogArchive.swift`). Phase 0 records identity-based before/after entry sets for the inherited TMPDIR and each contained test leg, so a new `wilted-*` entry fails even when older entries disappeared. The crash sweep removes only marked, inactive owned roots after its age cutoff. The audit attributes a new entry whose owner marker names a live process other than this run to that owner (`temp.foreign`), so a concurrent gate in another worktree is not this run's leak; an unmarked entry or one whose owner has exited is. `python3 scripts/check-no-global-tmp.py` checks operational scripts and docs, and in shell sources rejects `mktemp -t` and template-less `mktemp` (macOS puts both in the shared per-user temp root, ignoring `TMPDIR`) and any top-level `$TMPDIR/wilted-*` root that is not a `mktemp -d` directory marked by `wilted_temp_mark_owned` on the same line. `python3 scripts/check-log-conventions.py` checks that every shipped `Logger` uses a `com.zerodelta.wilted` subsystem and a PascalCase category, so `/usr/bin/log show --predicate 'subsystem BEGINSWITH "com.zerodelta.wilted" AND processImagePath BEGINSWITH "/Applications/"'` finds every installed app's lines. On every exit outside its self-tests, `scripts/test-gate.sh` runs `python3 scripts/sweep-test-preferences.py`, which removes the `com.zerodelta.wilted.mac.tests.*` and `com.zerodelta.wilted.mac.ui-fixture.*` plists of exited Mac test hosts from `~/Library/Preferences`; a running host keeps its own, and a reused PID does not count as running.
